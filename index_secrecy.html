Working notes Trade Secrets vs AI Transparency

Can an AI system be both a protected secret and an accountable one?

Secrecy is the default IP shield for AI. Model weights, training data and scoring logic are rarely patentable or worth publishing in a patent, so firms keep them as trade secrets. That same secrecy blocks the scrutiny that fairness, safety and public trust need. The law is moving to a controlled disclosure model: secrets stay protected from competitors, but regulators, courts and affected individuals can see enough to hold the system to account.

Takeaway for our topic

The DMA shows the same controlled-disclosure logic: mandated access, with anonymisation, audits, certification and use limits standing in for secrecy.

DMA clarification. The Digital Markets Act does not regulate stand-alone AI or set trade-secret rules. It bites when AI is built into a designated gatekeeper's services. No explicit trade-secret language was found in the DMA material read for these notes (secondary sources only; check the Commission decisions).

§5

Business and managerial implications

Disclosure planning

Secret by default is no longer safe by default

Plan for disclosure to regulators (documentation, training-data summaries, explanations) and keep it separate from what competitors can see.

Evidence of care

Show “reasonable steps” credibly

Access logs, NDAs, clean-room onboarding. X.AI v. OpenAI and Apple v. Liu turn on whether the plaintiff can identify specific secrets and prove misuse.

Patent vs secret

Choose what to publish

Patents publish the invention. Secrecy avoids that but invites transparency mandates and leaves no protection after a leak. A hybrid works:

Method→Patent
Weights→Trade secret
Data→Trade secret
Reputation & market access

Explainability is a competitive feature

EU fines (GPAI up to 3% of turnover or EUR 15m) and loss of public trust raise the stakes. Indian firms selling into the EU must comply regardless of India's lighter rules.
